# 9. Build community ownership

A community becomes more resilient when members do more than participate in activities organised by someone else.

Over time, members should have opportunities to:

- shape priorities
- contribute ideas
- organise activities
- facilitate discussions
- support other members
- create resources
- lead projects
- welcome newcomers
- represent the community
- help decide what happens next

The aim is not to transfer every responsibility away from a coordinator.

The aim is to create a community where **responsibility can be shared**.

---

# 9.1 Recognise the difference between participation and ownership

A member can participate without feeling any responsibility for the community.

For example:

> Attend → listen → leave

Ownership looks different:

> Participate → contribute → initiate → support others → help shape the community

Ask:

> **Are members helping to create the community, or mainly consuming what organisers provide?**

# 9.2 Create small opportunities to lead

Do not wait for someone to volunteer to become a major community leader.

Create small, manageable opportunities.

For example:

- facilitate a discussion
- host a short session
- welcome new members
- introduce two people
- curate resources
- organise an informal gathering
- lead a working group
- write a community update
- mentor another member
- coordinate one activity

Small responsibilities can help members build confidence and discover that they can shape the community.

# 9.3 Make opportunities visible

People may not volunteer simply because they do not know that help is needed.

Instead of:

> "Let us know if you'd like to get involved."

Try:

> "We need two people to facilitate next month's discussion. Each person would need to contribute approximately one hour."

Make opportunities:

- specific
- manageable
- time-bound
- visible
- supported

---

# 9.4 Match responsibility with support

Delegating responsibility does not mean abandoning someone.

If a member takes on a role, provide what they need to succeed.

This might include:

- a template
- an example
- a briefing
- access to relevant tools
- a co-facilitator
- a named contact for questions
- feedback afterwards

A useful principle is:

> **Give people enough support to succeed, while leaving enough space for them to make the role their own.**

---

# 9.5 Create member-led activities

Look for activities that can gradually move from organiser-led to member-led.

### Stage 1 — Organiser-led

The organiser designs and facilitates the activity.

### Stage 2 — Member-supported

A member contributes part of the activity.

### Stage 3 — Co-created

The organiser and members design the activity together.

### Stage 4 — Member-led

Members design and deliver the activity with the organiser providing infrastructure and support.

This does not need to happen for every activity.

The goal is to create **multiple pathways towards ownership**.

# 9.6 Develop community champions

Some members naturally become highly engaged.

They may:

- regularly answer questions
- connect people
- organise activities
- welcome newcomers
- share resources
- encourage others

These members can become important sources of community energy.

However, avoid creating an informal hierarchy where a small group becomes the only route into the community.

Instead:

- recognise their contribution
- invite them to take responsibility
- support them
- create opportunities for others to participate too

---

# 9.7 Avoid creating a permanent inner circle

Strong relationships are valuable.

But communities can become difficult to enter when experienced members form a closed group.

Watch for signs such as:

- the same people making every decision
- newcomers rarely speaking
- opportunities circulating among existing leaders
- informal knowledge being unavailable to others
- members referring to "the usual people"
- new members feeling like outsiders

Ask:

> **Who gets opportunities in our community?**

> 

> **Who doesn't?**

> 

> **How can we make pathways into deeper participation more visible?**

> 

---

# 9.8 Rotate responsibilities where appropriate

Rotating responsibilities can:

- distribute workload
- develop new skills
- reduce dependency on individuals
- bring different perspectives
- create leadership opportunities

Possible responsibilities to rotate include:

- facilitation
- meeting chair
- community updates
- welcoming
- event organisation
- working-group leadership
- resource curation

Not everything needs to rotate.

Some responsibilities benefit from continuity.

---

# 9.9 Build a culture of peer support

A mature community increasingly helps members support one another.

For example:

### New member

> "I'm trying to do X. Has anyone done this before?"

### Experienced member

> "Yes. Here's what we learned."

### Another member

> "We tried something different. Here's why."

The value is not simply the answer.

It is the **exchange of experience**.

Encourage members to explain:

- what they did
- why they did it
- what happened
- what they would change
- what others should know

This helps the community develop shared practice.

---

# 9.10 Recognise contribution without creating competition

Recognition can encourage contribution.

It might involve:

- thanking members
- highlighting useful contributions
- inviting members to share their work
- acknowledging behind-the-scenes effort
- celebrating community achievements
- giving members opportunities to represent the community

But be careful about turning contribution into a competition.

The aim is:

> **"Your contribution matters."**

Not:

> **"You need to outperform other members."**

# 9.13 Ask members what they want to own

Do not assume what members want to lead.

Ask them.

For example:

> **What would you like to contribute to this community?**

> 

> **Is there an activity you would like to organise?**

> 

> **Is there something you think the community should develop?**

> 

> **Would you like to support another member?**

> 

> **What would make it easier for you to get involved?**

---

# 9.14 The ownership check

Every few months, ask:

### Who initiated something recently?

> 

### Who organised something?

> 

### Who helped another member?

> 

### Who welcomed someone new?

> 

### Who made a decision?

> 

### Who created something useful?

> 

### Who is taking responsibility without being asked?

> 

If the answers are always the same few people, your community may be overly dependent on a small leadership group.

---

# 9.15 Know when to step back

As members become more capable, the organiser's role may change.

Early on, you may need to:

- initiate activities
- recruit members
- coordinate communication
- establish infrastructure

Later, you may spend more time:

- connecting people
- supporting member leaders
- maintaining infrastructure
- noticing gaps
- facilitating transitions
- protecting the community's purpose
- helping resolve difficult issues

The objective is not to become invisible.

It is to make the community increasingly capable of generating activity and value through its members.
