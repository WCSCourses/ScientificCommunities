# Practical Toolkit

<div class="toolkit-intro">

<p class="toolkit-purpose"><strong>Practical tools for designing, launching, facilitating and sustaining a scientific Community of Practice.</strong></p>

<p>Use these worksheets and canvases to turn the ideas in the guide into a practical plan for your own community.</p>

</div>

## How to use the toolkit

You do not need to complete every tool, and you do not need to use them in a fixed order.

Start with the questions that matter most to your community right now. As your Community of Practice develops, return to the tools and update them.

<div class="toolkit-meta">

<div class="toolkit-meta-box">
<strong>Working online</strong>
Complete the tools directly in your browser and use them as prompts for discussion with your team or community.
</div>

<div class="toolkit-meta-box">
<strong>Working offline</strong>
A downloadable participant workbook will bring the tools together so you can work through them digitally or on paper.
</div>

</div>

# Tips for using the toolkit

### Work with other people

These tools are often most useful when completed collaboratively. Use them to start conversations rather than simply to document decisions.

### Do not aim for perfect answers

Your community will change as you learn more. Treat the tools as living documents.

### Start with the real need

Especially when designing activities or infrastructure, begin with what members need to do rather than what you can offer them.

### Look for member contribution

A Community of Practice depends on interaction between members. Ask regularly what members are contributing, not only what they are receiving.

### Revisit the tools

The same canvas may look different six months after launch. That is a sign that the community is developing.
