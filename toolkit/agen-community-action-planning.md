# AGEN Community Action Planning

## Take ownership of the network

> **The infrastructure already exists. The question is: what will we do together to make AGEN work?**

The Asia Genomics Education Network (AGEN) brings together educators, trainers, researchers and bioinformatics practitioners working to strengthen genomics education and training across the Asia–Pacific region.

AGEN already has the foundations of a community:

- a network of trained practitioners and trainers
- standardised GSA teaching resources and delivery guidance
- a platform for sharing resources, events and opportunities
- a proposed community champion structure
- channels for communication and peer support

This workshop focuses on the next step: **turning these foundations into an active, useful and increasingly self-sustaining trainer network.**

---

## Workshop purpose

By the end of the workshop, participants will have contributed to a practical plan for the first 90 days of AGEN's development.

The plan should identify:

- the activities that will make the network useful
- how the Core Team, Community Champions and Trainer Network will work together
- what members can take ownership of
- how trainers will be supported to deliver GSA training locally
- how experience and feedback will flow back into the community
- the first actions that can begin immediately after the course

# DAY 1 — IMAGINE THE NETWORK

**Duration: 30 minutes**

### Purpose

Move from thinking of AGEN as something that has been created for participants towards thinking of it as **a network that members actively shape and contribute to**.

---

## 1. Reframe AGEN

### Facilitator introduction

AGEN already exists.

We have a platform where members can connect, share resources and events, and access information. We have standardised GSA teaching resources and delivery guidance. We have people who have already been trained.

But a platform and a collection of resources do not automatically create an active community.

**The next step is to decide what happens between courses — and who makes it happen.**

AGEN's goal is to cultivate a self-sustaining network of trained practitioners and trainers across the Asia–Pacific region who can deliver high-quality, standardised Genome Sequence Analysis bioinformatics training at scale.

# CHALLENGE 2 — WHO OWNS AGEN?

**30–60 minutes**

AGEN has three tiers:

### CORE TEAM

Sanger / GSA course instructors

**Role:** Maintain standardised resources, set the quality bar and coordinate the community.

### COMMUNITY CHAMPIONS

Selected experienced past trainers/participants

**Role:** Mentor other trainers, co-lead onboarding and act as regional points of contact.

### TRAINER NETWORK

Past course participants

**Role:** Apply resources in their own institutions, deliver training locally and feed back their experience.

## Facilitator notes

### The role of the facilitator

The facilitator's role is to help participants make decisions, surface different perspectives and keep the conversation focused on practical action.

Avoid solving every question for the group.

Where possible, ask:

> "What do you think?"

> "Who should own that?"

> "What would make this sustainable?"

> "Would members actually contribute to this?"

> "What could happen without the Core Team doing it?"

### Keep the distinction clear

AGEN already has infrastructure and resources.

The workshop is **not** asking participants to redesign the platform or recreate the resource hub.

Instead, focus on:

**People → Relationships → Activities → Ownership → Training → Feedback**

### Watch for these traps

**"We need more resources."**

Ask:

> What specific problem would that resource solve?

**"The Core Team should organise it."**

Ask:

> Could a Community Champion or trainer own this instead?

**"We should have more meetings."**

Ask:

> What would the meeting enable people to do?

**"Everyone should participate."**

Ask:

> What would make participation valuable enough for people to want to contribute?

**"Let's create a new platform/tool."**

Ask:

> Could we use the existing AGEN platform to do this?

### Keep the action plan small

The objective is not to produce an exhaustive strategy.

A successful workshop should leave the group with:

- **3 or fewer major priorities**
- clear ownership
- specific first actions
- realistic deadlines
- a small number of indicators of progress

The network can evolve from there.
