<h3>Worked example</h3>

<p>Imagine you are developing a regional Community of Practice for people who use and teach genomic data analysis. You might start with a small number of activities that create different ways for members to learn, share, solve problems and contribute.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Activity</th>
<th>What happens?</th>
<th>Who is it for?</th>
<th>How often?</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>1. Community problem-solving session</strong></td>
<td>Members bring a technical or practical challenge and work through it together.</td>
<td>All members, particularly practitioners looking for peer support</td>
<td>Monthly</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>2. Skills workshop</strong></td>
<td>A member or invited expert leads a practical session on a useful tool, method or workflow.</td>
<td>Members who want to develop specific skills</td>
<td>Every 2–3 months</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>3. Peer exchange / discussion</strong></td>
<td>Members share experiences, approaches and lessons learned around a common topic.</td>
<td>All members</td>
<td>Monthly</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>4. Mentoring / peer support</strong></td>
<td>More experienced members provide advice and support to less experienced members.</td>
<td>Early-career members and those seeking specific guidance</td>
<td>Ongoing</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>5. Community showcase</strong></td>
<td>Members present a project, resource, training activity or solution they have developed and invite discussion.</td>
<td>All members</td>
<td>Quarterly</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p><strong>Remember:</strong> A Community of Practice does not need a large programme of events. Start with a small number of activities that address genuine member needs and create opportunities for people to learn from and contribute to one another.</p>
