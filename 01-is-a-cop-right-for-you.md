# 1. Is a Community of Practice right for you?

Before creating a community, start with a simple question:

> **What problem would become easier to solve if people learned and worked together?**

A Community of Practice (CoP) is not simply a mailing list, professional network, discussion forum or group of people who happen to have the same job title.

A CoP brings together people who:

- share a meaningful domain of interest or practice
- interact and learn from one another
- develop knowledge, resources, experiences and approaches together
- have a reason to continue interacting over time

A useful way to understand a CoP is through three interconnected elements:

**Domain + Community + Practice**

These three elements need to develop together.

---

## The quick test

Answer the questions below.

### 1. Do people share a domain?

Is there a specific area of scientific practice, knowledge or challenge that connects the people you want to bring together?

For example:

- genomic surveillance
- single-cell analysis
- clinical bioinformatics
- scientific community engagement
- reproducible research
- AI applications in genomics

**If no:** define the domain before going further.

---

### 2. Is there something members can learn from one another?

Could members benefit from sharing:

- experience?
- methods?
- tools?
- lessons learned?
- problems and solutions?
- opportunities?
- resources?
- local knowledge?

**If no:** a broadcast communication channel or information resource may be more appropriate.

---

### 3. Do members need one another?

Would members be able to achieve something better together than individually?

For example:

> "We can each run training independently, but together we can develop shared training resources, mentor new trainers and strengthen genomics capacity across the region."

**If no:** reconsider whether a community is the right mechanism.

---

### 4. Is there a reason to interact regularly?

A CoP develops through sustained interaction.

Ask:

> **What would bring members back next month?**

Possible answers include:

- solving problems together
- peer consultation
- regular seminars
- working groups
- collaborative projects
- mentoring
- resource development
- discussing new developments
- sharing opportunities
- troubleshooting difficult technical issues

**If you cannot identify a reason for continued interaction, stop here and revisit the purpose.**

---

### 5. Are you willing to create a space where members contribute?

A community is not an audience.

Members should have opportunities to:

- ask questions
- share experience
- contribute resources
- teach one another
- shape priorities
- lead activities
- collaborate
- influence the direction of the community

**If your model depends mainly on one organisation producing content for everyone else, you may be building an audience rather than a CoP.**

---

# The CoP test

Use the table below to assess your idea.

| Question | Yes | Partly | No |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| We have a clear shared domain | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Members can learn from one another | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Members need one another | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| There is a reason to interact regularly | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Members can actively contribute | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| We can identify a meaningful shared purpose | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

### What does your result tell you?

**Mostly Yes**

You have the foundations of a potential CoP. Continue to Step 2.

**Several Partly**

You may have a promising idea, but some elements need clarification before you launch.

**Several No**

Do not rush into creating a community. Consider whether another mechanism — such as a network, mailing list, project team, training programme or information resource — would better address the need.

## Before you continue

You should now be able to answer:

- Who is this community for?
- What is its domain?
- What do members have in common?
- What can they learn or create together?
- Why does this require a community rather than simply a website or mailing list?
- Why would members continue participating?

If you cannot answer these questions yet, spend more time here.
